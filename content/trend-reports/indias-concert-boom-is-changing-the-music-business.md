# India's Concert Boom Is Changing the Music Business

The streaming era made music easier to access.

The concert boom is making it valuable to leave the house for.

India's organised live-events segment grew 44% in 2025, according to FICCI-EY. That made live events one of the fastest-growing parts of the country's media and entertainment economy.

For music, the implications go beyond ticket sales.

Live performance is becoming one of the most important ways artists turn digital attention into physical revenue.

## The audience is bigger than the metros

One of the most revealing findings from EY's research into Diljit Dosanjh's Dil-Luminati India tour was that nearly half of attendees came from Tier-II and Tier-III cities.

More than 320,000 people attended the tour across 13 cities.

That changes the old assumption that major concerts are primarily a Mumbai, Delhi and Bengaluru story.

India's music audience is geographically much broader, and audiences are increasingly willing to travel for artists they care about.

## A concert creates an economic chain

The ticket is only the beginning.

A large concert can generate spending across hotels, flights, trains, taxis, restaurants, retail, event production, security, venue infrastructure and local employment.

EY's research around large-format concerts has highlighted a roughly 3x economic multiplier from concert activity through these wider effects.

That is why cities are beginning to see concerts differently.

A major show is not simply an entertainment event. It can become tourism.

## Artists are building touring businesses

For independent artists, this is particularly important.

Streaming can produce reach. Touring can produce revenue and deeper fandom. The two systems reinforce each other.

An artist who has built an audience digitally can now sell tickets without waiting for a film soundtrack, television appearance or major-label push.

The result is a different career model.

Release music. Build an audience. Tour. Return with new music. Tour again.

The artist becomes the business rather than simply the performer inside someone else's business.

## The infrastructure still has to catch up

India's appetite for concerts is growing faster than its concert infrastructure.

Venues, production equipment, permissions, transport, crowd management and event logistics all have to scale alongside demand.

That is why companies are increasingly moving beyond ticketing and into infrastructure, event production and venues.

The next stage of India's concert economy may therefore be defined not only by who sells the most tickets, but by who builds the infrastructure capable of supporting the demand.

## The live show is becoming part of the artist identity

A great recording can create a listener.

A great live show can create a fan.

As streaming becomes increasingly crowded, live performance gives artists something algorithms cannot fully replicate: a shared physical experience.

For India's next generation of artists, touring may become as important as releasing.

**The country's music business is discovering that sometimes the strongest connection happens after the headphones come off.**
