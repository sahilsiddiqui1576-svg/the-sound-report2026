# Regional Music Is Becoming India's Growth Engine

For decades, the Indian music industry had a centre of gravity. Hindi film music sat at the middle of the national conversation, while regional industries developed enormous audiences of their own.

Streaming is changing the geometry.

The biggest opportunity in Indian music may no longer be making one song that everyone in the country understands. It may be making a song that deeply belongs to one place and allowing technology to carry it everywhere else.

## The numbers are changing

Spotify's 2026 Loud & Clear report shows how significant the shift has become.

Royalties generated by Telugu-language music grew more than 120% year over year in 2025. Marathi grew nearly 50%. Bengali grew nearly 40%. Malayalam grew more than 30%. Tamil grew 20%.

Then there is Haryanvi. Streams of Haryanvi music on Spotify increased nearly sevenfold between February 2023 and February 2026.

That is not simply a regional trend. It is a distribution story.

## The audience was already there

The easiest mistake is to describe this as streaming platforms creating regional audiences.

They did not.

The audiences already existed.

What digital platforms have changed is the ability to find, recommend and monetise those audiences at scale.

An artist no longer needs to relocate to Mumbai to reach listeners outside their immediate geography. A song can remain culturally specific while becoming digitally universal.

## Language is becoming an advantage

A decade ago, language could be viewed as a barrier to national reach. Today, it can become part of the identity of a song.

Punjabi music has demonstrated this particularly well, while Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi and Haryanvi music are finding increasingly visible audiences beyond their traditional markets.

Streaming recommendations do not require listeners to understand every word before pressing play.

Sometimes the sound arrives first. The meaning follows.

## The industry is responding

Platforms and music companies are building infrastructure around the change, from regional editorial programmes to dedicated language-focused artist development and label strategies.

That matters because regional music is no longer a niche to be managed. It is becoming a portfolio of growth markets.

## India's music map is getting bigger

The most interesting development is not that regional music is replacing Hindi music.

It is that the definition of mainstream Indian music is expanding.

A listener in Mumbai can discover a Haryanvi track. A listener in Delhi can fall into Telugu pop. A listener outside India can discover Punjabi, Tamil or Malayalam music without ever needing a traditional Indian music-industry gatekeeper.

The country is moving from one national music market toward dozens of interconnected ones.

And that may be one of the biggest opportunities Indian music has ever had.

**India's regional diversity is no longer something the music industry has to overcome. It is becoming the industry's advantage.**
