# India's Music Economy Is Growing, But Streaming Is Only Part of the Story

For years, the Indian music business could be explained through one dominant word: streaming.

That is no longer enough.

India's media and entertainment industry grew 9% in 2025 to ₹2.78 trillion, according to the FICCI-EY report released in March 2026. Within that larger economy, the music sector grew 10%. But the more interesting change is where artists and music companies are finding value.

Music is becoming a broader business than simply putting songs on streaming platforms.

## Streaming is still the foundation

Paid music subscriptions in India grew 37% in 2025 to approximately 14.4 million. The audience itself is much larger, with roughly 178 million online music streamers.

That gap is now one of the industry's biggest opportunities.

The EY-IMI study released in July 2026 found that 96% of smartphone users in India listen to music, while 80% spend more than an hour a day doing so. India has the listeners. The industry is still figuring out how to turn more of those listeners into sustainable music consumers.

## The money is moving beyond the song

The modern music business increasingly looks like an ecosystem.

An artist can generate value through streaming, live performances, brand partnerships, social media, publishing, merchandise, sync licensing and direct fan relationships.

A song may begin on Spotify, gain attention through Instagram, build a following on YouTube and eventually become a ticket-selling mechanism for a live show.

The recording is no longer necessarily the final product. It can be the beginning of the business.

## Live music changes the equation

India's organised live-events segment grew 44% in 2025, according to FICCI-EY.

For musicians, that changes the value of having an audience. A streaming listener is valuable. A listener who buys a ticket, travels to another city, buys merchandise and brings friends is part of a much larger economic relationship.

That is why India's concert economy matters to recorded music too.

## The next phase is about monetising fandom

India does not have a music consumption problem. It has a value-conversion problem.

The audience already listens at enormous scale. The next stage is convincing people that music is worth paying for, attending in person, supporting directly and building a relationship around.

That could make the next chapter of Indian music less about how many people listen to a song and more about how many different ways an artist can build value around that audience.

**The Indian music economy is getting bigger. But it is also getting wider.**
