# The Algorithm Is Becoming India's New Music Gatekeeper

There was a time when getting heard meant getting selected.

A radio programmer selected the song. A television channel selected the video. A label selected the artist. A film producer selected the soundtrack.

Streaming changed the equation.

Now another gatekeeper sits quietly between an artist and a listener.

The algorithm.

## Discovery is becoming personalised

Spotify has continued expanding tools built specifically around music discovery, from personalised playlists to more controls over what appears in discovery surfaces.

The direction is obvious.

The question is becoming less: **What is popular?**

And more: **What is relevant to me?**

## That changes the artist's problem

For an emerging musician, the challenge used to be getting into the room.

Today, the challenge is getting into the recommendation loop.

A song can be discovered by a listener who has never heard the artist's name. That listener saves it. The platform learns. The recommendation expands. More listeners arrive. The song creates more signals. The cycle continues.

Spotify reported that listeners played an Indian artist they had never heard before 12.8 billion times in 2025. Indian artists generated nearly 335 billion streams on the platform overall.

That is an enormous discovery machine.

## Playlists are becoming infrastructure

Playlists are no longer simply collections of songs. They are distribution systems.

An editorial playlist can introduce an artist to an audience they could never have purchased access to. A personalised playlist can introduce the same artist to one listener at exactly the right moment.

Traditional promotion asks people to pay attention. Recommendation technology tries to find people who are already likely to care.

## The algorithm does not replace taste

There is a danger in making the algorithm sound more powerful than it is.

Algorithms can surface music. They cannot manufacture genuine connection.

The Indian artists seeing major growth are still making songs with distinctive identities. Technology helps reduce the distance between the song and the listener. The music still has to create the reason to stay.

## The new gatekeeper is also a new opportunity

Traditional gatekeepers could say no once.

An algorithm can keep discovering.

A song released months ago can suddenly find the right audience. An artist without a major label can accumulate listeners before the industry decides they matter.

That is one reason independent music is gaining ground.

Streaming has not removed gatekeepers. It has multiplied them.

But some of those new gatekeepers are systems that can introduce an unknown artist to a listener without asking for permission from the old music establishment.

**The future of discovery may belong less to whoever gets the biggest launch and more to whoever keeps getting recommended after the launch is over.**
